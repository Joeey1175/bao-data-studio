Beauty & Skincare Competitive Market Analysis¶

Project Overview¶

This project evaluates six selected skincare brands using Sephora product data to understand competitive positioning across pricing, product portfolios, and customer response.

Business Questions¶

  1. How do competitors differ in pricing strategy?
  2. Which brands maintain focused or diversified portfolios?
  3. How does customer response vary across competitors?
  4. Where might opportunities for differentiation exist?

Dataset¶

Source: Sephora Products and Skincare Reviews (Kaggle)

Scope: 304 skincare products across six selected brands.

Tools¶

Python, Pandas, Matplotlib

Data Cleaning Decisions¶

  • No duplicate product IDs were identified.
  • All 304 products contained valid price information and were retained for pricing and portfolio analysis.
  • Nine products had missing rating and review information. These products were retained in the master dataset but excluded from customer-response analyses requiring rating or review metrics.
  • Missing tertiary category values were retained because secondary category information was complete and sufficient for the primary portfolio analysis.
  • No products were removed solely based on high prices, as premium pricing may represent legitimate brand positioning rather than a data-quality issue.

Initial Pricing Observation¶

The six competitors show two clearly differentiated pricing groups. The Ordinary and The INKEY List occupy an accessible price position, with median product prices of $10.00 and $12.99, respectively. In contrast, the four remaining competitors have median prices ranging from $34.75 to $46.00.

Kiehl's Since 1851 has the highest median price at $46.00, while The Ordinary has the lowest at $10.00. Price distributions also indicate substantially broader price ranges among the higher-priced brands.

4. Key Findings & Strategic Recommendations¶

The competitive analysis reveals distinct strategies across pricing, portfolio structure, and customer response. The following findings summarize the most important competitive patterns and their potential strategic implications.

Finding 1 — The market shows a clear pricing divide¶

The Ordinary and The INKEY List occupy distinctly accessible price positions, with median product prices of $10.00 and $12.99, respectively. The remaining four competitors have substantially higher median prices ranging from $34.75 to $46.00.

Recommendation:
A brand entering or repositioning within this competitive set should establish a clear price position rather than competing broadly across both accessible and higher-priced segments. Pricing should reinforce the intended brand proposition and product strategy.

Finding 2 — Portfolio specialization is a major source of differentiation¶

The Ordinary demonstrates the strongest category specialization, with 73.3% of its portfolio concentrated in Treatments. The INKEY List is also treatment-focused at 46.3%, while brands such as CLINIQUE, Kiehl's Since 1851, and Drunk Elephant maintain more diversified portfolios.

Recommendation:
Brands should determine whether differentiation is better supported by category specialization or broader skincare coverage. A focused portfolio can create a clearer product identity, while diversification may support a wider range of customer needs.

Finding 3 — Customer response varies by competitive strategy¶

No single competitor leads across all customer-response metrics. Paula's Choice records the highest average product rating at 4.40, Drunk Elephant generates the highest average review volume per rated product, and The Ordinary generates the highest average Sephora loves per product at approximately 147K.

Recommendation:
Competitive performance should be evaluated using multiple customer-response measures rather than a single metric. Ratings can indicate product satisfaction, while review activity and Sephora loves provide additional signals of customer interaction and interest.

Finding 4 — Portfolio whitespace does not automatically indicate demand¶

Treatments are the most heavily represented core category, with 95 products, but also generate the highest average Sephora loves per product. Sunscreen is comparatively underrepresented—particularly among accessible-positioned competitors—but shows weaker customer-response metrics within the available data.

Recommendation:
Potential portfolio expansion should consider both competitive whitespace and evidence of customer interest. Underrepresented categories should be validated with additional demand, trend, or sales data before being treated as growth opportunities.

Conclusion¶

The analysis demonstrates that the six selected skincare competitors follow meaningfully different strategies across pricing, portfolio structure, and customer response.

The Ordinary and The INKEY List compete through accessible pricing and treatment-focused portfolios, while several higher-priced competitors maintain broader category coverage. Customer-response patterns further demonstrate that strong ratings, review activity, and customer interest do not necessarily occur within the same brands.

For a skincare brand evaluating its competitive position, the results suggest that differentiation should be built around a deliberate combination of price position, portfolio focus, and customer value proposition rather than any single competitive dimension.

The analysis also highlights the importance of distinguishing competitive whitespace from demonstrated market opportunity. Additional sales, market-size, and consumer-demand data would be required before making investment or market-entry decisions.
